01RESEARCH

Where should we grow?

Competitive analysis of the prepaid telecom market in Thüringen: 13 carriers across 15 cities.

  • The market is split by channel. Big 4 carriers sell through own stores and retailers, discounters through supermarkets, ethnic carriers through ethnic shops and kiosks.
  • No ethnic carrier sells in supermarkets. That gap became a recommendation: pilot a supermarket partnership in Erfurt.
  • Lebara and Ortel are the direct threats, competing for the same migrant communities on international-minute bundles.

02CAMPAIGNS

Which campaigns pay off?

Return on investment and conversion for 12 campaigns across 96 partners, March 2024 to May 2025.

  • B2B engagement campaigns return about twice as much as product launches: around 61% ROI against around 30%.
  • Poster + SMS converts better for 3 of 4 partner segments. In-store visits with flyers win only with Premium partners.
  • Premium partners cluster at €13,000–20,000 revenue; At-Risk partners stay under €4,000.

03FUNNEL

Where do we lose partners?

The B2B sales funnel from first visit to active partner, plus acquisition channels and revenue growth.

  • 46% of approached shops become active partners (91 of 198).
  • The biggest loss is the first step: 27% of shops never reach a demo, against 5% lost between onboarding and activation. The fix is a stronger first-visit pitch.
  • These conversion rates set the win probabilities in the HubSpot pipeline (stage 05), so the CRM forecast is based on history, not guesses.

04RETENTION

Who needs a visit this week?

A churn-risk score for every partner, built from visit frequency, revenue trend and NPS, turned into a weekly visit list.

  • Each partner gets a risk score and category (critical, high, medium, low) with a recommended action.
  • The weekly visit list ranks shops by revenue at risk, so field time goes where losing a partner would cost most.
  • Retention is tracked monthly against a 95% target, with NPS alongside.

05OPERATION

Making it run every day: HubSpot CRM and Make

The analysis only matters if the sales team acts on it. I built the operating layer: a CRM pipeline and an automation that runs on its own.

HubSpot CRM. A 5-stage partner pipeline with win probabilities, custom company properties for segment, churn risk and acquisition channel, an active segment that flags partners needing a visit, a DSGVO-compliant lead form, and a dashboard with a weighted revenue forecast.

Make automation. Every day, Make calls the HubSpot REST API with a scoped service key and finds deals that reached Onboarding. For each new one it adds a row to an onboarding log in Google Sheets and emails the rep an onboarding checklist.

  • No duplicates: a data store keyed on the HubSpot deal ID remembers every processed deal, so repeated runs never log or email twice.
  • Failures are visible: if the API call fails, an error route sends an alert and marks the run as failed.

I also completed the Salesforce Administrator learning path on Trailhead, so the same CRM setup carries over to Salesforce. See my Trailhead profile.
